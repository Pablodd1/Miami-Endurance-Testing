TRAINING ZONES EXPLAINED

Z1

Recovery (50-60% MHR)

Physiology

Pure aerobic metabolism using fat as primary fuel. Blood lactate remains at resting levels. Minimal cardiovascular stress. Increases capillary density and mitochondrial multiplication.

Benefits

  • Enhances fat oxidation
  • Promotes recovery
  • Builds aerobic base
  • Increases capillary density
Z2

Aerobic Base (60-70% MHR)

Physiology

Mix of fat and carbohydrates. Below aerobic threshold (LT1). Heart stroke volume increases. Mitochondrial biogenesis peaks. Foundation for all endurance performance.

Benefits

  • Primary endurance building zone
  • Increases fat oxidation capacity
  • Enhances cardiac stroke volume
  • Marathon pace development
Z3

Tempo (70-80% MHR)

Physiology

The gray zone. Lactate rises above baseline but is cleared efficiently. Increasing glycogen use. Sustainable for 1-3 hours. Creates fatigue without optimal stimulus.

Benefits

  • Improves lactate clearance
  • Builds muscular endurance
  • Develops sustained race pace
  • Time trial preparation
Z4

Threshold (80-90% MHR)

Physiology

Lactate threshold (LT2) or FTP. Lactate accumulates faster than cleared. Heavy carbohydrate reliance. Sustainable 30-60 minutes. Norwegian method focus zone.

Benefits

  • Increases lactate threshold
  • Improves FTP and power
  • Enhances VO2max
  • 30-60 min race pace
Z5

VO2max (90-100% MHR)

Physiology

Maximum anaerobic effort. Body cannot supply oxygen fast enough. Rapid lactate accumulation. Trained through 30sec-8min intervals. Builds aerobic ceiling.

Benefits

  • Increases VO2max capacity
  • Improves anaerobic capacity
  • Top-end speed and power
  • Finishing kick
